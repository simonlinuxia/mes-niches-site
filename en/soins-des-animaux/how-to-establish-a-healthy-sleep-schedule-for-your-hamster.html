Hamster Sleep: How to Support a Healthy Day and Night Rhythm

Published on October 5, 2026

Photo: Ellie Burgin / Pexels

Hamsters sleep most of the day and wake up in the evening. You cannot change that rhythm, but you can help your hamster get the rest it needs. A well-placed cage, a calm room and a regular routine do most of the work.

Understand how hamsters sleep

Hamsters are most active at dusk and during the night, and they sleep in several short periods during the day. A hamster that sleeps while you are at work is perfectly normal. Waking it up often is what causes stress.

Choose the right spot for the cage

Put the cage in a quiet room, away from the television, loud speakers, slamming doors and busy hallways. Keep it out of direct sunlight and away from heaters and drafts: hamsters overheat easily, and a room that is too cold can make them sluggish. A steady room temperature of about 18 to 24 °C (65 to 75 °F) suits most hamsters.

Keep light and noise natural

Normal daylight and darkness are enough. At night, avoid bright lights near the cage. Do not play loud music or use the cage as a nightlight. If the cage is in your bedroom, remember that the wheel is noisy at night: choose a quiet wheel or move the cage to another room.

Give it a place to sleep

  • a hiding house or hut where it can retreat in the dark;
  • deep bedding made of paper or aspen, with no cedar or scented products;
  • a solid-surface wheel of the right size so its back stays straight.

Plan the day around its rhythm

Handle your hamster in the evening, when it wakes up naturally, and let it come to you. Clean the cage when it is awake, and put fresh food in the evening, since that is when it eats. Avoid waking it up to play.

Signs that something is wrong

See a vet who treats small animals if your hamster sleeps and does not react at all, stops eating or drinking, has wet or dirty fur around the tail, has diarrhea, loses weight or seems cold and stiff. Hamsters hide illness, so small changes matter.

A simple routine

Evening: greet it, give fresh food and water, spend a few minutes together. Night: let it run and chew. Day: keep the room calm and let it sleep.
