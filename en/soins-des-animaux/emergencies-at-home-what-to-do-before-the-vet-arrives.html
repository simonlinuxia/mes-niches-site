Emergencies at Home: What to Do Before the Vet Arrives

Published on September 19, 2026

When your pet is in distress, the seconds can feel like hours. Whether it's a sudden illness, an injury, or a suspected poisoning, knowing what to do before you can get to a veterinarian can be crucial. This guide provides practical steps you can take to help stabilize your pet until professional help arrives.

Immediate Assessment

First, assess your pet's condition. Look for signs of distress such as difficulty breathing, severe bleeding, or loss of consciousness. Make a quick decision based on your observations. If your pet is severely injured or in a life-threatening situation, do not delay in seeking professional help. Call your vet or local animal emergency clinic right away.

Keeping Calm

It’s crucial to remain calm. Your pet can sense your anxiety, which can increase their stress. Speak softly and keep the environment quiet and safe. If your pet is showing signs of distress, try to keep them still to prevent further injury. For example, if your dog has a suspected broken leg, immobilize the limb gently without applying direct pressure to the break.

Basic First Aid

Wound Care: If your pet has a small, clean cut, you can gently clean the area with warm water and a mild antiseptic solution. However, avoid using hydrogen peroxide, as it can damage tissue. Do not apply anything too harsh, and stop if your pet becomes agitated.

Stop Bleeding: Gently apply firm but gentle pressure to the wound with a clean cloth or bandage. Do not use tourniquets unless instructed by a professional, as they can cause more harm than good.

Burns: If your pet has a burn, do not apply any ointments or creams. Instead, gently rinse the area with cool water (not ice-cold) and cover with a clean, damp cloth.

Administering Medications

Do not give your pet human medications or over-the-counter remedies without consulting your veterinarian first. Incorrect dosages or medications can be harmful. If your vet has prescribed medications, follow the instructions carefully.

Pain Management

If your pet is in pain, some over-the-counter pet-safe pain relievers like meloxicam may be used, but only under the guidance of a veterinarian. For severe pain, it’s better to wait for professional intervention.

Monitoring Vital Signs

Keep an eye on your pet’s breathing, pulse, and temperature. If you notice any changes, alert the vet immediately. For breathing, watch for abdominal movement or listening for sounds. Pulse can be felt by pressing gently on the femoral artery on the inside of the thigh. Normal temperature ranges can vary, so check with your vet for specific guidance.

Creating a Comfortable Environment

If your pet is in pain or showing signs of distress, moving them can be stressful. If possible, keep your pet in a quiet, dark area. Use a blanket or a soft pillow to create a comfortable bed. Avoid rough handling or loud noises.

Contacting Veterinary Services

While waiting for professional help, keep your pet’s medical history handy. This includes any medications, allergies, and previous health issues. Write down any symptoms or changes in behavior. Provide this information to the vet as soon as possible.

Conclusion

Emergencies with pets can be scary and stressful. By being prepared and knowing what to do, you can provide critical support to your pet until professional help arrives. Remember, the key is to act quickly, stay calm, and prioritize your pet’s well-being.

Always keep a list of emergency numbers, including your vet’s contact information, nearby animal hospitals, and pet poison control. Regular check-ups and keeping your pet up-to-date with vaccinations can also help prevent many emergencies.
