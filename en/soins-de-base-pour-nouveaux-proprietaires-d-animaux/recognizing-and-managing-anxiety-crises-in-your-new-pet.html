Recognizing and Managing Anxiety Crises in Your New Pet

Published on October 5, 2026

Photo: beauQ / Pixabay

As a new pet owner, you're excited to welcome your furry friend into your home. However, it’s important to be prepared for the possibility of your pet experiencing anxiety. Anxiety can manifest in various ways, and knowing how to recognize and manage these crises is crucial for both your pet’s and your own peace of mind. Here are seven key steps to help you manage anxiety crises in your new pet.

Identify the Signs of Anxiety

Anxiety in pets can look different from one animal to another. Common signs include:

  • Excessive panting or heavy breathing
  • Excessive drooling
  • Excessive grooming (such as licking the paws)
  • Hiding or avoiding contact
  • Whimpering or whining
  • Urinating or defecating inappropriately

For example, if you notice your cat frequently hides under the bed or your dog starts panting heavily during car rides, these could be signs of anxiety.

Create a Calm Environment

Creating a calm environment can significantly reduce your pet’s anxiety. Ensure your home is free from loud noises and sudden movements. For instance, if you have a pet that gets anxious during thunderstorms, use white noise machines to mask the sound and provide a secure space with soft blankets or a cozy bed.

Regular Exercise

Exercise is crucial for managing anxiety in pets. It helps to release endorphins, which are natural mood elevators. Aim for at least one to two 30-minute walks per day for dogs. For cats, regular play sessions with toys can help maintain their physical and mental health.

Positive Reinforcement Training

Teach your pet positive behaviors using positive reinforcement techniques. This not only helps in training but also strengthens the bond between you and your pet. For example, if you notice your dog gets anxious in certain situations, like when you leave the house, start training them to associate those situations with positive experiences, like a treat or a toy.

Use Calming Aids

Consider using calming aids designed for pets. These can include pheromone sprays, calming collars, or natural supplements. For instance, a calming collar can be used during car rides to reduce your dog’s anxiety. Always consult with your vet before introducing any new products to ensure they are safe for your pet.

Consult with a Professional

If your pet’s anxiety persists or worsens, consult with a veterinarian or a professional animal behaviorist. They can provide tailored advice and, if necessary, prescribe medication to help manage your pet’s anxiety. For example, a behaviorist might recommend desensitization and counterconditioning techniques to address specific fears your pet might have.

Manage Stressors

Identify and manage any stressors that might be contributing to your pet’s anxiety. This could include changes in routine, new household members, or other pets. For example, if you recently adopted a new kitten, ensure the existing cat is given adequate space and time to adjust. This can be achieved by keeping them in separate rooms initially and gradually introducing them when they both seem relaxed.

Practice Consistency

Consistency is key in managing anxiety in pets. Stick to a routine that includes feeding, playtime, and bedtime. This helps your pet feel secure and reduces anxiety. For instance, establish a regular feeding schedule and stick to it, even when you’re out.

By following these seven steps, you can help your new pet manage anxiety crises effectively and ensure they have a happy and healthy life. Remember, every pet is unique, so tailor these suggestions to fit your specific circumstances and consult with professionals when necessary.
