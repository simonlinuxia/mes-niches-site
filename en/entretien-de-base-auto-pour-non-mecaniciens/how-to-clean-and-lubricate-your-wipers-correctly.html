How to Clean Your Wipers and Know When to Replace Them

Published on September 27, 2026

Photo: Andrea Piacquadio / Pexels

Clear visibility in rain, snow and road spray depends on wiper blades in good condition. A few minutes of cleaning each month keeps them working longer, and knowing the warning signs tells you when cleaning is no longer enough and it is time for new blades.

Why clean your wipers

Dust, road film, tree sap and insects build up on the rubber edge of the blades. Instead of sweeping the water away, a dirty blade smears it, and the streaks reduce your visibility just when you need it most. Clean blades also wear more slowly.

Tools and materials needed

  • A lint-free or microfiber cloth
  • Windshield washer fluid or isopropyl (rubbing) alcohol
  • Car glass cleaner or warm soapy water
  • A second clean cloth for drying

Step-by-step guide

  1. Lift the wiper arm away from the windshield until it stays upright. Hold it with your hand so it cannot snap back: a falling arm can crack the glass.
  2. Dampen the cloth with washer fluid or isopropyl alcohol and wipe the rubber edge of the blade from one end to the other. Repeat with a clean part of the cloth until it comes away clean.
  3. Clean the windshield itself with glass cleaner or soapy water, then dry it. A dirty windshield soils a clean blade again within a few passes.
  4. Lower the arm gently back onto the glass.
  5. Do not put oil, silicone spray or any other lubricant on the rubber. It makes the blade smear and can reduce your visibility.

How often should you clean them?

Once a month is a good habit, and again whenever you notice streaks. In winter, clear snow and ice from the windshield before turning the wipers on, and never use them to scrape ice: it tears the rubber. In summer, parking in the shade protects the rubber from drying out.

When to replace the blades

Most blades last about 6 to 12 months, depending on the climate and how much you drive. Replace them when:

  • they still leave streaks or smears after cleaning;
  • the rubber is cracked, split or hardened;
  • the blade skips or chatters across the glass;
  • the frame is bent or a piece is missing.

Wiper blades are inexpensive and, on most cars, easy to change. Check your owner’s manual or the parts store’s guide for the right size and fitting.

Practical example

You notice streaks every time it rains. You clean the rubber edge with alcohol and wash the windshield, and the streaks disappear. Three months later they come back and the rubber looks cracked: it is time for a new pair of blades.

Conclusion

Cleaning your wipers takes five minutes and keeps them working longer, but cleaning cannot repair worn rubber. If the wipers still leave streaks with new blades, or if the arms or the wiper motor do not move properly, have a mechanic check them: good visibility is a safety matter.
