Easy-to-Maintain Indoor Plants for Beginners: A Beginner’s Guide

Published on September 19, 2026

An "easy" plant depends on your home and habits more than on the species. Here is how to choose according to light, your routine and any pets.

1. Look at the light you have

Watch the light near your windows for a few days. Near a bright window, nearly any houseplant will do. In a dimmer corner, choose a snake plant, ZZ plant or pothos, which tolerate low light.

2. Match the plant to your routine

If you often forget to water, snake plants, ZZ plants, jade plants and succulents are forgiving. If you like checking in on your plants, spider plants, pothos and ferns need a little more regular care.

3. Think about pets

Several popular plants are toxic to cats and dogs: pothos, philodendron, monstera, ZZ plant, snake plant, peace lily, jade plant and aloe. Spider plants and peperomia are generally considered safer. If your pet chews plants, keep them out of reach and check with your vet before buying.

4. Think about size and pot

A small nursery plant will grow, so plan for space. A pot that is too large keeps soil wet for too long. Always pick a pot with a drainage hole.

5. Buy a healthy plant

  • firm leaves with no spots or insects;
  • soil neither soggy nor bone dry;
  • roots not bursting out of the drainage holes.

6. Ignore exaggerated claims

Houseplants do not purify indoor air in a measurable way. Choose them because you enjoy them.

Fertilizing and repotting

Feed lightly during spring and summer, following the label, and not at all in winter. Repot only when roots fill the pot, usually every two or three years.

For a first plant, a spider plant, pothos or snake plant is an excellent choice depending on your light and pets. Start with one, learn what it needs, then add more.
